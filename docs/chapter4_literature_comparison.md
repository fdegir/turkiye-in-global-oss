# Chapter 4 literature comparison and interpretive boundaries

This is a **comparison of empirical constructs**, not a pooled meta-analysis. The selected 42-project LFX data and the sources below have materially different populations, observation periods and definitions.

| Source | Main empirical or conceptual finding | Relationship to this study | Non-comparable dimension |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wachs et al. (2022), *The Geography of Open Source Software: Evidence from GitHub* | OSS activity diffuses internationally but remains geographically clustered within countries | Independent G20 rank comparison confirms broad alignment: ρ = 0.950877 for 19 countries | ≥100 commits in 2019–2020; active GitHub accounts vs LFX project-occurrences |
| Rossi & Zacchiroli (2022), *Geographic Diversity in Public Code Contributions* | Long-run regional diversification in 2.2bn Software Heritage commits (1971–2021) | Shows why geography and time horizon matter | Authors/commits, world regions, multi-decade longitudinal data |
| Juhász, Wachs, Kaminski & Hidalgo (2026), *The software complexity of nations* | Programming-language specialisation yields a software economic-complexity indicator associated with macro outcomes | Supports studying the economics of OSS; participation–GDP ρ = 0.720 is directionally compatible | Complexity, specialisation and relatedness are not participation counts |
| Osborne & Lawson (2025), LF Research *Open Source as Europe's Strategic Advantage* | High adoption coexists with uneven organisational strategy and underinvestment in sustaining OSS | Context for separating individual from organisational participation | Survey evidence, not the same contributor/project population |
| Gineikyte-Kanclere, Eggert & Skiotyte (2025), European Parliament, *European software and cyber dependencies* | Reliance on external software and cloud providers creates exposure | Supply-chain dependencies are not captured by national contribution volume | Provider dependence, substitution and infrastructure control |
| Thabit Gonzalez et al. (2026), JRC, *Digital sovereignty metrics: A multi-layer approach* | Digital sovereignty spans governance, software/data/infrastructure, markets and people | This study offers partial evidence for only some software-participation and people dimensions | A contributor ranking is not a sovereignty score |

## Interpretation

**Corroborated:** Country ordering in the predefined G20 comparison is strongly associated with the earlier Wachs et al. dataset. This supports the plausibility of the ranking pattern within those two operationalisations, not the accuracy of any specific unknown-country attribution.

**Complementary:** Associations with GDP and R&D fit an economic-capability interpretation but are non-causal and conceptually narrower than the complexity literature. Project-level criticality and health are not measures of country control.

**Not measured:** National maintainer authority, project governance voting rights, dependency exposure, security response capability, capability to sustain or fork critical projects, market power or procurement exit options. Conclusions about Türkiye's strategic autonomy must remain carefully qualified.
